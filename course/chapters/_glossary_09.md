- **scaling law** — an empirical power-law relationship between a model's loss and its parameters N, training tokens D, or compute C, holding across many orders of magnitude (Ch. 09)
- **iso-FLOP curve** — the loss of models that all cost the same compute C, plotted against model size; U-shaped, with the best model at the bottom (Ch. 09)
- **compute-optimal frontier** — the line through the minima of the iso-FLOP curves: the best (N, D) for every budget (Ch. 09)
- **FLOP** — one floating-point operation (a multiply or an add); training compute is counted in FLOPs (Ch. 09)
- **6·N·D rule** — training compute ≈ 6 FLOPs per parameter per token: 2 forward, 4 backward (Ch. 09)
- **MFU** — model FLOPs utilisation: the useful 6·N·D FLOPs achieved divided by the hardware's peak FLOP/s; ~40% is good for a large run (Ch. 09)
- **Chinchilla** — the 2022 DeepMind study finding that compute-optimal training scales N and D together, about 20 tokens per parameter (Ch. 09)
- **irreducible loss E** — the constant floor in L = E + A/N^α + B/D^β: the entropy of the text itself, which no model can beat (Ch. 09)
- **overtraining** — training a model on far more tokens than the compute-optimal count (e.g. 1,900 tokens/parameter for Llama-3 8B) to get a smaller, cheaper-to-serve model (Ch. 09)
- **inference-aware scaling law** — a scaling law that minimises training FLOPs plus expected lifetime serving FLOPs (2·N per served token) (Ch. 09)
- **muP** — Maximal Update Parametrisation: scaling initialisation and per-layer learning rates with width so the optimal learning rate is width-independent (Ch. 09)
- **hyperparameter transfer** — tuning learning rate and related settings on a small proxy model and reusing them at scale, enabled by muP (Ch. 09)
- **loss curve** — training or validation loss plotted against steps or tokens; read on a log-token axis for its power-law shape (Ch. 09)
