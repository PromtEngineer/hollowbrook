- **language model** — a function from a sequence of tokens to a probability distribution over the next token (Ch. 01)
- **probability distribution** — a set of non-negative numbers, one per possible outcome, that add up to 1 (Ch. 01)
- **token** — the unit of text a model reads and predicts; a word or punctuation mark in Chapter 1, a learned sub-word piece from Chapter 2 on (Ch. 01)
- **context** — the tokens that come before the position being predicted (Ch. 01)
- **maximum-likelihood estimate** — the parameter choice that makes the training data most probable; for an n-gram it is the plain count ratio count(c, w) / count(c) (Ch. 01)
- **n-gram model** — a language model whose context is the previous n − 1 tokens, estimated by counting (Ch. 01)
- **bigram** — an n-gram model with one token of context (n = 2) (Ch. 01)
- **trigram** — an n-gram model with two tokens of context (n = 3) (Ch. 01)
- **Markov assumption** — the (false but useful) assumption that only the last n − 1 tokens affect the next one (Ch. 01)
- **smoothing** — any method that moves a little probability mass from seen events to unseen ones so nothing has probability exactly zero (Ch. 01)
- **add-k smoothing** — smoothing by pretending every possible (context, next) pair was seen k extra times (Ch. 01)
- **chain rule of probability** — P(w₁…w_T) = ∏ P(wᵢ | w₁…wᵢ₋₁): a sequence's probability is the product of each token's probability given the tokens before it (Ch. 01)
- **log-probability** — the logarithm of a probability; turns the chain-rule product into a sum and avoids underflow (Ch. 01)
- **bits** — the unit of −log₂ P; a probability-½ event costs 1 bit (Ch. 01)
- **surprisal** — −log₂ P(token | context): how surprised the model was by the token that actually came (Ch. 01)
- **cross-entropy** — the average surprisal per token of a model on a text; the training loss of every neural language model (Ch. 01)
- **perplexity** — 2 raised to the average bits per token; the effective number of equally likely choices per step; lower is better (Ch. 01)
- **effective branching factor** — the plain reading of perplexity: how many options the model is, on average, choosing between (Ch. 01)
- **held-out data** — text kept out of training and used only for measurement (Ch. 01)
- **generalization** — how well a model does on data it was not trained on, as opposed to memorising its training data (Ch. 01)
- **sampling** — choosing the next token at random in proportion to its probability (Ch. 01)
- **temperature** — a knob that reshapes the distribution before sampling: below 1 sharpens it toward the top token, above 1 flattens it (Ch. 01)
- **greedy decoding** — always choosing the highest-probability next token; deterministic and prone to loops (Ch. 01)
- **sparsity** — the problem that the number of possible contexts grows exponentially with context length, so most are never observed in training (Ch. 01)
