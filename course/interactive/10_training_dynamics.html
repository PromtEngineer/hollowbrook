Training dynamics Chapter 10

A real optimiser on a real (tiny) network, live in your browser: watch how the learning-rate schedule, warmup, batch size and clipping shape the loss curve.
What to try. Press Loss spike: the learning rate is too high, the loss shoots up and the model gets stuck with saturated units. Then press one of the fixes — warmup, gradient clipping, or a lower peak LR — and compare the curves. Switch the schedule between cosine, WSD and constant to see the LR curve and the final-loss difference.
What is simulated. This is not an LLM. It is a 2-hidden-layer MLP (2→16→16→1, tanh, 289 parameters) trained with minibatch SGD-momentum or AdamW on a 400-point 2-D "inside the circle?" task, with the exact lr_at() schedules from llm/optim.py. Loss is cross-entropy, gradients are computed by hand-written backprop, nothing is faked. The shapes — warmup preventing early blow-ups, spikes from too-large steps, clipping bounding the damage, cosine vs WSD tails — transfer to LLM training; the numbers do not.

Challenge. Start from the Loss spike preset (SGD, peak LR 1.5, no warmup, seed 1). Without touching the peak LR, clipping or batch size, find a warmup length that removes the spike and reaches a full-data loss below 0.01 by step 600. Then try seed 2 and 3 with your warmup: does it still hold?
Show answerWith seed 1, warmup of 50–100 steps trains cleanly (max loss ≈ 0.7, final ≈ 0.001), while 0 or 25 steps spike above 1 and get stuck. Warmup works because at initialisation the gradients are large and the loss surface is sharp; ramping the LR lets the weights reach a flatter region before the big steps arrive. It is not a guarantee: on this task a longer warmup (200) can still spike for some seeds, because the cosine schedule then holds the LR near 1.5 for hundreds of steps and one unlucky minibatch is enough. Clipping at 1.0 is the more robust fix here — exactly why LLM runs use warmup and clipping and a conservative peak.
