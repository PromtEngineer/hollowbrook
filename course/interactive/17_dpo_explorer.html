Move the four log-probabilities and β; watch the implicit rewards, the margin, the loss and its gradient — then take real gradient steps.

What to try. Drag log π(rejected) down and watch the loss fall while the gradient σ(−β·margin) shrinks — DPO stops pushing once the pair is well separated. Then press One gradient step a few times: chosen goes up and rejected goes down at first, but with a long, similar rejected answer (dials at the bottom) both can go down while the loss still improves. That is the "DPO only cares about the difference" lesson.

Log-probabilities of a whole response are sums over its tokens, so −25 nats is a typical value for a short answer (e.g. 25 tokens at 37 % each). The sliders are the four numbers dpo_loss in llm/dpo.py receives.

Read: the loss only sees the difference Δ. Any change that leaves Δ alone — lowering both answers by the same amount — leaves the loss untouched.

One gradient step (first-order model of an SGD step — see below)

Let gc = ∇θ log π(c) and gr = ∇θ log π(r). One SGD step is θ ← θ − η∇loss = θ + ηβσ(−βΔ)(gc − gr). To first order, log π(c) then moves by ηβσ(−βΔ)·(gc·gc − gc·gr) and log π(r) by ηβσ(−βΔ)·(gc·gr − gr·gr). With ‖gc‖ = 1 the two dials fix these dot products. Similar answers (high overlap) with a longer rejected answer (larger norm — a sum over more tokens) make both numbers negative: Δ still grows, so the loss still falls. This is a linearised model, not a real network, but the sign pattern is the one seen in practice.

DPO:   loss = −log σ( β·Δ ),  Δ = log-ratio(c) − log-ratio(r)
IPO:   loss = ( Δ − 1/(2β) )²
SimPO: loss = −log σ( β·Δavg − γ ),  Δavg = avg log π(c) − avg log π(r) (per token, no reference)

These are dpo_loss, ipo_loss and simpo_loss from llm/dpo.py. Same x-axis for all three, but note what "margin" means: for DPO/IPO it is the difference of log-ratios against the reference; for SimPO it is the difference of length-averaged log-probabilities, with no reference model at all. DPO keeps asking for a bigger gap (the loss never reaches 0); IPO wants the gap to land exactly on 1/(2β) and penalises overshooting; SimPO demands a gap of at least γ/β before it is satisfied.

Example: log πref(c) = log πref(r) = −25, log πθ(c) = −27, log πθ(r) = −47, β = 0.1. Then r̂(c) = −0.2, r̂(r) = −2.2, Δ = 20, β·Δ = 2 and loss = −log σ(2) = 0.127. Both answers lost probability (chosen by 2 nats, rejected by 22) — the mass moved to other responses the loss never looks at. Real DPO runs show exactly this: the chosen log-probability usually drops during training; only the gap grows. Or take steps with overlap 0.9 and norm ratio 1.6: each step lowers both, because (gc·gc − gc·gr) = 1 − 1.44 < 0.
