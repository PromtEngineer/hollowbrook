Prompt cache

Stable-prefix rule: a provider can reuse the KV-cache for the longest prefix that is byte-identical to a previous request. Append-only histories cache well; anything that rewrites earlier content (compaction, a date in the system prompt, re-ordered tool schemas) invalidates everything after the first changed byte. Cached input tokens are billed at roughly a tenth of the price, which is why harnesses put the stable material first and compact rarely and deliberately.

Context rot — illustrative curve of task accuracy versus how full the window is; the shape is a sketch, not measured data. That performance degrades as relevant material is buried under more tokens is the reported finding (e.g. Chroma "Context Rot", 2025; arXiv 2606.29718 on long-horizon search agents); the numbers depend on model and task.

Two settings each solve it on their own. (1) auto-compact on with keep_last = 6: every time usage crosses 25.6k the older tool results collapse to 12-token stubs; the window then holds the task, ~30 short assistant turns, the stubs and the last few full results — it ends somewhere between ~13k and ~26k depending on when the last compaction fired, and peaks at about 27.5k (the check runs after the turn that crossed the line, as in Agent.run). (2) Truncation to 2 000 chars: every result is capped at ~500 tokens, so 30 calls cost ~17k and no compaction is needed — but the model only sees the head and tail of each result. In an 8k window neither is enough alone (three untruncated 3k results already overflow), so you need truncation and compaction, or sub-agents that return one summary instead of many results. The general rule: budget = fixed cost + what you must keep verbatim + what you can afford to lose.
