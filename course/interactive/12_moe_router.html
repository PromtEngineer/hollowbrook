MoE router Chapter 12

Tokens are routed by a softmax over expert logits to their top-k experts. Watch load balance — or collapse — under a small simulated training loop.
What to try. Press Train with the auxiliary loss off: experts that happen to win early get better at their tokens, attract more of them, and the rest starve — routing collapses onto one or two experts. Reset, switch the aux loss on, and train again: the load bars level out. Drag an expert's affinity slider to see how a single bias term moves traffic.
What is simulated. The router is real: logits = W·x + b, softmax, top-k, and the Switch-Transformer auxiliary loss E·Σe fe·Pe exactly as in llm/model.py::MoE; router weights are updated by real gradient descent on (expected token loss + α·aux). What is a stand-in: each expert's "skill" on a token type grows with how many of those tokens it has processed — a one-line model of the rich-get-richer dynamic that causes routing collapse in real MoE training.

Challenge. Make one expert receive 0 tokens, then fix it without touching that expert's affinity slider. Explain, from the formula for the aux loss gradient, why the fix works.
Show answerTwo ways to starve an expert: drag its affinity to −4 (its logit loses every top-k contest), or train with the aux loss off and let collapse do it. To fix it without its slider: turn the aux loss on and train. The gradient of E·Σe fePe with respect to token i's logit for expert j is (E/n)·pij·(fj − Σe fepie): it pushes down logits of experts whose share fj is above the token's expected share and, through the softmax normalisation, pushes up the underused ones. Starved experts have f≈0, so every token's logit for them rises until they win some routes. (DeepSeek-V3 does this with a per-expert bias adjusted by a simple rule instead of a loss — "auxiliary-loss-free balancing" — which is exactly your affinity slider driven automatically.)
