    Challenge. Craft a spam document that passes the heuristic filter (no banned phrases, symbol ratio ≤ 0.1, no repeated lines or 3-grams, ≥ 5 words, enough English stopwords) but is dropped by the quality classifier (score < 0.5). Then explain why a learned classifier is used in addition to hand rules.
    Show answerSomething like "Get the best price on cheap laptops with free shipping and the lowest deals of the year. Order today and save with our coupon code." has plenty of stopwords, no banned phrase and no repetition, so every heuristic passes — but its vocabulary (price, cheap, shipping, deals, coupon, order) matches the classifier's "junk" training examples, so its score falls below 0.5. Hand rules catch structural junk (repetition, symbols, boilerplate strings); a classifier catches semantic junk that looks well-formed. FineWeb-Edu and DCLM do exactly this at scale: an LLM labels ~500k pages, a small model imitates it, and the small model scores billions of pages.
