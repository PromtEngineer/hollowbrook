Challenge. (1) On the bowl with plain SGD, find a learning rate at which the ball diverges (loss grows every step) and the largest one at which it still converges. (2) On the valley, find any optimizer + learning rate that reaches the minimum (1, 1) within 300 steps. Why is the bowl's threshold so sharp?
Show answer

The bowl is f = x² + 5y², whose steepest direction (y) has curvature 10. Plain gradient descent on a quadratic with curvature c multiplies the error each step by (1 − lr·c), so it diverges once lr·c > 2, i.e. lr > 0.2 here; at lr = 0.2 exactly it oscillates for ever and just below it (≈ 0.19) it converges, bouncing. Try lr ≈ 0.25 to see the explosion and lr ≈ 0.1 for a clean descent. In a real network the "curvature" is unknown and changes during training, which is why the learning rate is the most tuned hyperparameter.

On Rosenbrock, SGD needs lr around 0.001–0.002 and then thousands of steps to creep along the flat valley floor; anything above ≈ 0.003 blows up in the steep walls. Adam with lr ≈ 0.03–0.1 gets to (1, 1) in a few hundred steps because it normalises the step per coordinate. Momentum at lr ≈ 0.001 helps but overshoots along the valley.
