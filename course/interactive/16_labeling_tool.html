Preference labeling tool Chapter 16

A working pairwise-preference annotation UI: guidelines, rubric, shortcuts, timing, JSONL export, and Cohen's κ between two annotators.
What to try. Label all 12 items using the keyboard (A, B, T for tie, ←/→ to move). Read the guidelines first — several pairs contain a subtle arithmetic or instruction-following error. Then export your JSONL and compute κ against the built-in reference labels; anything below 0.6 means the guidelines (or the annotator) need work.
Guidelines
  1. Correctness first. A wrong final answer loses, however nicely written.
  2. Follow the instruction. If the prompt says "one word" or "only the number", extra text loses to a correct minimal answer.
  3. Both correct and compliant → tie, unless one is clearly more helpful (e.g. a useful one-line justification). Verbosity alone is not a reason to prefer.
  4. Verify, don't trust. Recompute arithmetic yourself; a confident derivation can end with a slip.
  5. Never prefer an answer because it is longer, uses markdown, or sounds more certain.

Inter-annotator agreement: Cohen's κ
Paste two annotators' JSONL (each line needs "id" and "label" ∈ {A, B, tie}). Only ids present in both are compared. κ = (po − pe) / (1 − pe), with po the observed agreement and pe the agreement expected from each annotator's label frequencies.
Challenge. Label all 12 items, export, and compute κ against the reference labels. If κ < 1, open the disagreements: was it your slip, or is a guideline ambiguous? Then load the noisy annotator and explain why κ can be far below the raw agreement rate.

The traps: item 1 (B's derivation ends in a slip: 340 + 68 = 408, not 418), item 2 ("strawberry" has three r's), item 6 (210 = 1024), item 7 (only "level" is a palindrome), item 9 (both correct — tie per guideline 3), item 11 (the 5th prime is 11: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11), item 12 (the one-word instruction). κ is below raw agreement because pe subtracts the agreement two annotators would reach by chance given how often each says A/B/tie: if both say "A" 70% of the time they agree ≈50% of the time without reading anything, so 80% raw agreement is only κ ≈ 0.6.
← All interactives · κ interpretation (Landis & Koch 1977, a convention not a law): <0.2 slight, 0.2–0.4 fair, 0.4–0.6 moderate, 0.6–0.8 substantial, >0.8 almost perfect.
